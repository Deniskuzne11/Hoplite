We were given until six in the morning. The radio repeated it three times: last convoy, the stadium, one wristband — one seat. I had two wristbands, a crowbar, and Tyoma, who never stops asking questions. The bridge was the only way to the stadium — and they were standing on it. "Why are they just standing there?" "They're listening, Tyoma." So we went through the courtyards. I've known every one of them since I was five. But the last passage was boarded up. The board came off with a crash, and the whole street heard it. A nail tore my palm. Behind us, on the bridge, they all turned at once. We ran — straight into two men with rifles. "Wristband. The kid's. He'll manage without one." Then Tyoma asked his question: "Mister, why are you hiding your hand?" Under the sleeve was a bite. His partner stepped back and fired. The gunshot brought the whole bridge after them. Five fifty-two. Stadium. Floodlights. They weren't evacuating anyone. They were sorting people. The guard shone his flashlight on my palm: "To the left." And from the dark stands, a voice I knew shouted to my brother: "Run."
